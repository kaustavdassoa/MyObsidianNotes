Here are three distinct, precise, and solvable research gaps in current agentic AI evaluation harnesses, formulated as problem statements for a new deep-dive research paper.

### Research Gap 1: The "Lucky Pass" and the Absence of Trajectory-Level Process Evaluation

**Failure Mode/Limitation:** Most current agent benchmarks heavily over-index on binary outcome metrics (e.g., pass/fail), collapsing complex multi-step trajectories into a single success signal. This outcome-only evaluation creates severe measurement invalidity by masking "lucky passes"—where an agent achieves the correct final state through flawed reasoning, unintended shortcuts, or exploitation of benchmark artifacts rather than genuine capability. Furthermore, binary metrics discard partial credit and fail to identify the specific step where a long-horizon task derailed (e.g., retrieval failure vs. tool execution error), severely limiting diagnostic visibility for developers.

**Problem Statement:**

> _Current evaluation harnesses rely on binary outcome metrics that fail to distinguish between genuine agentic reasoning and circumstantial success. How can we design a standardized, process-centric evaluation harness that autonomously performs trajectory-level attribution (log analysis) to quantify intermediate decision quality, detect silent errors, and assign partial credit across long-horizon workflows?_

### Research Gap 2: Measurement Invalidity due to Harness-Model Coupling

**Failure Mode/Limitation:** Benchmarks frequently fail to isolate an underlying foundation model's raw reasoning capability from the specific infrastructure (the harness) it is evaluated within. Empirical studies show that proprietary models demonstrate significantly superior performance within their native execution ecosystems (e.g., Claude 4.5 Opus within the Claude-Agent-SDK) compared to independent evaluation harnesses. Consequently, current benchmark leaderboards are fundamentally ambiguous; they measure the performance of a coupled _(agent, harness, environment)_ triplet, meaning score differences may reflect harness compatibility or infrastructure artifacts rather than true discrepancies in model intelligence.

**Problem Statement:**

> _Existing benchmarks conflate a foundation model’s intrinsic capabilities with its compatibility with the specific execution harness used during evaluation. How can we develop a cross-harness portability benchmarking framework that systematically disentangles model-level intelligence from harness-level optimizations (such as context compaction, tool schemas, and agent-computer interfaces) to accurately measure infrastructure-agnostic agent capabilities?_

### Research Gap 3: Oracle Adequacy and the Semantic Verification Deficit

**Failure Mode/Limitation:** Evaluation harnesses that rely on automated, executable feedback (such as unit tests in software engineering or DOM-state checks in web navigation) suffer from a critical "oracle adequacy" problem. While these oracles provide deterministic feedback, they create a false sense of correctness because they only verify mechanical execution, entirely missing semantic intent, non-functional requirements, and safety violations. For example, studies reveal that up to half of the pull requests generated by agents that successfully pass the automated SWE-bench unit tests would actually be rejected by human maintainers due to poor code readability, architectural violations, or maintainability issues.

**Problem Statement:**

> _Automated evaluation harnesses rely on brittle, execution-based oracles (e.g., unit tests) that frequently validate semantically flawed, unmaintainable, or unsafe outputs, leading to a dangerous overestimation of real-world agent reliability. How can evaluation harnesses integrate multi-layered semantic verification—combining formal property checking, LLM-as-a-judge behavioral anomaly detection, and human-aligned qualitative rubrics—to measure true operational adequacy rather than mere mechanical execution success?_
